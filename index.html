01

Research

I study how compact objects respond to their environments and how that response can reveal both the dynamics of general relativity and possible new physics.

Black-hole response

I use black-hole perturbation theory and effective field theory to characterize the response of compact objects to external perturbations, including tidal interactions, scattering, absorption, and dissipation.

Black-hole perturbation theory

I am interested in perturbations of Schwarzschild and Kerr black holes, with particular emphasis on gauge-invariant observables, horizon dynamics, and finite-frequency response.

Quantum structure & strong-field observables

A broader goal of my work is to understand whether microscopic modifications of classical black holes can leave observable signatures in gravitational-wave or scattering experiments.

My earlier work studied black-hole microstates, horizonless extremely compact objects, and black-hole thermodynamics using tools from string theory and conformal field theory.

About

I completed my PhD in Physics at The Ohio State University in 2025. My doctoral research focused on black-hole thermodynamics, horizonless extremely compact objects, black-hole microstates, and the information problem.

Before that, I received an integrated B.S.–M.S. degree in Physics from IISER Bhopal.

I am originally from Jaipur, India.
